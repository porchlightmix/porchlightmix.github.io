How to release an acoustic version of a song you already put out

Porchlight Mix · October 29, 2026 · 6 min read

You've already released a song, and now you've got a stripped-down take: just you and a guitar, or a piano and a harmony. It's also where a lot of upload questions start. Do you need a new ISRC? What do you call it? Can you pitch it on Spotify? Will it show up for your followers?

The answers are spread across distributor help pages, Apple's style guide and the ISRC rulebook. Here they are in one place.

The short answer

  • It's a new recording, so it gets a new ISRC. Your distributor can assign one for free.
  • Title it with the version in parentheses: "Song Title (Acoustic Version)". Leave the original's title alone.
  • Upload it as a new track, not as a replacement for the original's audio.
  • Pitch it on Spotify before release day, but don't count on Release Radar for it.
  • Master it to suit the song, not to match the loudness of the full-band version.

Yes, it needs its own ISRC

An ISRC (International Standard Recording Code) identifies one specific recording, not the song itself. The official ISRC Handbook says identical recordings share an ISRC and different ones get different codes. Its test is creative input: if creative input is what makes two recordings different, a new ISRC is required. It also says different studio takes are different recordings "even if all the personnel are the same."1

CD Baby's help center puts it in plain terms: a new performance or recording of an existing song generally needs a new ISRC, while an unchanged recording keeps the same one on a single, an album or a compilation.2 An acoustic re-recording is clearly the first case.

A few related points that trip people up:

  • The song stays the same. CD Baby notes that an ISRC identifies the recording, while an ISWC identifies the composition, so two recordings of one song can point to the same ISWC.2 You're releasing a new recording of a song you already wrote, not a cover of yourself.
  • A new export isn't a new version. The handbook says a high-resolution file (say, 24-bit/96 kHz) and a standard-resolution file of the same recording carry the same ISRC.1 Re-exporting the original mix at a different bit depth doesn't make it "acoustic."
  • You don't need to buy codes. DistroKid says it generates an ISRC for every song you upload that doesn't already have one, free and automatically.8

How to title it

Put the version in parentheses after the song title. CD Baby says each version must have a unique title that clearly identifies how it differs from the original, with the version info in parentheses, and it lists "Acoustic version" among accepted labels.3 DistroKid asks you to type the bare song title in the title box and use its separate "Add 'version' info to song title?" option, which lists Acoustic as a common version.6 Either way, listeners see something like Porch Light (Acoustic Version).

Apple Music's style guide adds some rules worth knowing:

  • Don't relabel the original. The standard version of a track shouldn't carry extra title info such as "Album Version" or "Original Mix."5
  • Spell it out. Apple asks for standard spellings with no abbreviations in track version info.5
  • Same arrangement, recorded again? That's a different case. Apple says a re-recorded track must include "Rerecorded" in its title, and may be hidden for editorial reasons if the original "hit" version is live.5 Use "Acoustic Version" when the arrangement really is acoustic.

Match the artwork too. CD Baby says your metadata should match your art: if the cover says "Live," the title should too.3 If your acoustic cover says "Acoustic," make sure the title does.

Upload it as a new track, not a swap

Some distributors let you replace the audio on a release that's already out. DistroKid's Audio Swap (Ultimate plan) is meant for small fixes and mastering tweaks, and DistroKid says you can't use it for remixes, alternate versions or new recordings.7 So the acoustic version goes up as its own upload, with its own ISRC and its own stream count.

Avoid two mistakes:

  • Don't send the same audio twice under a new name. Apple says it may hide content when copies of the same audio are submitted with slightly different titles.5 Calling the original mix "acoustic" to get a second release is exactly that.
  • If you include the original on the same release, keep it identical. CD Baby says that when a recording moves to a new package, its artist name, track title, version and ISRC should stay consistent, and stores may reject or hide releases that look like duplicates or have conflicting details.4

For the release date, give the acoustic version its own. Apple's style guide says re-recorded versions take the date the new version was released, and previously unreleased material takes the date it first became available, not the date it was recorded.5

Spotify: pitching and Release Radar

Spotify lets you pitch upcoming, unreleased songs to its editors in Spotify for Artists. Once a song has gone live it's no longer eligible, and you can only pitch one song at a time.10 Your original can't be pitched again, but a new acoustic recording that hasn't been released yet is a new upcoming song. Spotify asks you to deliver at least 7 days before release so editors have time to listen. Pitching never guarantees a playlist.10

The catch is Release Radar, the Friday playlist your followers get. Spotify's Release Radar page says it doesn't include some alternative versions of tracks, "like acoustic, karaoke, or live versions," and doesn't include re-releases of songs already on Spotify. It does include remixes.9 So don't build your plan around followers finding the acoustic version in Release Radar. Tell your listeners about it yourself.

Don't push it louder to match the full-band version

An acoustic arrangement is usually quieter and more dynamic than a full-band one, and that's fine. Spotify plays tracks at about −14 LUFS by default. On an album, it normalizes the whole album at once, so "the softer tracks are as soft as you intend them to be." On a playlist or on shuffle, each track is adjusted on its own.11

So crushing a fingerpicked version to match the band version gains nothing on a playlist and costs you its dynamics. Spotify's own tips are to keep true peak below −1 dBTP, or below −2 dBTP if the master is louder than −14 LUFS.11 Our guides to how loud an acoustic song should be on Spotify and why a song can sound quieter on Spotify go deeper.

Acoustic version upload checklist

  • ☐ It's a genuinely new recording, not the original mix re-exported
  • ☐ New ISRC (let your distributor assign one), with the original's ISRC left unchanged
  • ☐ Title: "Song Title (Acoustic Version)", using the distributor's version field if it has one
  • ☐ Original title left as it was, with no "Original" or "Album Version" added
  • ☐ Artwork wording matches the title
  • ☐ Same songwriter credits as the original song
  • ☐ Release date is the new version's own date
  • ☐ Delivered at least 7 days early and pitched in Spotify for Artists
  • ☐ Loudness and true peak checked, without chasing the full-band version's level
