Generic mix advice can hurt an acoustic song

A lot of mix advice assumes a loud, bass-heavy pop or EDM sound: big sub-bass, bright highs, loudness pushed to the limit. Follow that on a fingerpicked ballad or a worship set, and it can work against the song.

Genre-blind advice (example)

"Low end is 6 dB below target. Boost sub-bass. Increase loudness to −8 LUFS."

On an acoustic track, that leaves you with boom and a squashed vocal. And Spotify turns a −8 LUFS master down by about 6 dB anyway.

A Porchlight Mix note (example)

"Your vocal drops about 3 dB under the strummed guitar in the choruses. Try a little vocal ride or a gentle 2–4 kHz dip on the guitar."

Notes compared with reference recordings in your style, in words you can act on in any recording app.

Frequently asked questions

What is Porchlight Mix?

Porchlight Mix is an online mix check for acoustic and vocal music. It measures your finished mix or master and tells you, in plain English, what to fix before you release it on Spotify, Apple Music and other streaming services.

Who is it for?

Independent singer-songwriters, home recordists, folk and country artists, small bands and church worship teams who mix their own songs and want to know if they're ready to release.

How much does it cost?

The basic Release Check is free and available now. The full report is $4.99 per song, and you can pre-order one today. The Songwriter Plan will be $9.99 a month for 10 full reports, starting at launch.

What's the difference between the free check and the full report?

The free check measures your file: loudness, true peak, clipping, silence and format, and what each streaming service will do with it. The full report also looks at the mix itself: how the lead vocal sits against the band, tone balance compared with songs in your style, stereo width and mono, with a prioritized fix list.

Is my song uploaded when I use the free check?

No. The free check runs entirely in your web browser. Your file never leaves your device, and we never see it or its results.

What free tools do you have?

Three, and they all run in your browser. The Release Check measures loudness, true peak, clipping and format. The Loudness Preview shows, and lets you hear, how much each streaming service will turn your song up or down. The Release Checklist builds a dated to-do list from your release date and distributor.

When should I upload my song and pitch it to Spotify?

Most distributors suggest uploading about 3–4 weeks before release. Pitch to Spotify's editors in Spotify for Artists at least 7 days before release. Our free Release Checklist works out the dates for you, with links to each distributor's own rules.

How loud should my song be for Spotify?

Spotify turns every song to about −14 LUFS on its default setting, so a louder master just gets turned down. Spotify recommends a true peak below −1 dBTP, or below −2 dBTP if your master is louder than −14 LUFS. Many acoustic releases sound good somewhere around −16 to −12 LUFS. Read more in How loud should an acoustic song be for Spotify?

What file types can I use?

The full report accepts WAV, AIFF, FLAC and MP3. The free check works with WAV, FLAC and MP3 in every modern browser, and with AIFF and M4A in some browsers. For the most accurate result, use the same lossless file you'll send to your distributor.

Do I need to install anything?

No. The free check runs in your browser. For the full report, you export a mix from any recording app (or even a phone recording app) and send us the file.

How long does a full report take?

Usually 2–3 days. We email it to you as a PDF with a short fix list. It isn't instant, because each report is prepared one at a time during early access.

Is a human listening to my song?

No. Your report is generated automatically from measurements of your audio and written up in plain English. Your song stays private.

What happens to my song after the report?

We delete uploaded songs within 7 days of upload, along with any working copies we made. The report itself is kept so you can ask about it later. See our privacy policy.

Will you share my song or use it to train AI?

No. Never. Your song is never public and is never shared with anyone. It's never sent to an outside AI service, and it's never used to train any AI model. We analyze it with our own software on our own computer (this includes an open-source audio-separation tool that splits the vocal from the instruments). No human listens to it.

Does it work for mastered tracks too?

Yes. Check a mix before mastering, or a final master before you send it to your distributor.
